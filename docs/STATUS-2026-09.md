## The headline

**The router works. The experts have never mattered. Four designs, same result.**

`scripts/expert_intervention.py` forces a fixed routing policy and re-validates.
The `none` column is **every expert switched off**:

| design | data | gate | **none** | force:wrong | **spread** |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 (`cond3d`) | fog2 | 0.7457 | **0.7456** | 0.7450 | **0.0011** |
| 3 (`cond3h`) | fog2 | 0.7451 | **0.7461** | 0.7442 | **0.0020** |
| 3 (`cond3h`) | night | 0.7315 | **0.7316** | 0.7318 | **0.0010** |
| 3i (`cond3i`) | fog2 | 0.7476 | **0.7473** | 0.7473 | **0.0013** |
| 3i (`cond3i`) | night | 0.7346 | **0.7346** | 0.7336 | **0.0017** |

**`cond3i` is the informative one.** Its per-expert reconstruction losses worked:
the restoration gain went from **−0.0368 to +0.0453** on fog and **−0.0095 to
+0.0284** on night, with 81–83% of images improved, and `expert_share` rose from
0.047 to 0.320. The experts now provably move degraded features towards their
clear twin — and the detector still does not care. **Feature restoration and
detection are decoupled.** See `results-cond3i-perexpert.md`.

Switching the entire mixture off costs nothing, on any condition, in any design.
Forcing the *wrong* expert costs nothing. The block is a dense model carrying an
expensive unused branch.

## Inference cost — the efficiency claim does not hold

No TTA, fp32, 640, CUDA-synced, real test images:

| run | params | GF static | **GF activated** | ms @b1 | **FPS @b16** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `union3h_dense` | 2.594 M | 6.52 | 6.52 | 8.37 | **1375.1** |
| `cond3d` (design 2) | 3.014 M | 9.23 | 6.10 | 10.42 | 772.9 |
| `cond3h` (design 3) | 3.278 M | 11.19 | **7.62** | 9.91 | **696.2** |

Design 2 at least did *less* arithmetic than dense (6.10 vs 6.52) while running
slower. **Design 3 does more arithmetic AND runs at half the throughput.** The
encoder–decoder fog expert costs what an encoder–decoder costs, and the
masking/gather/`index_add` machinery costs more than the convolution it skips.

## What design 3 changed, and what it bought

*(Superseded in part by `cond3i` — the restoration row below describes the
block-level term. Applied per expert instead, the same objective reversed sign:
fog −0.0368 → +0.0453. See `results-cond3i-perexpert.md`.)*

Four interventions, all aimed at the measured causes of inertness:

| change | rationale | result |
|---|---|---|
| heterogeneous architectures (fog = encoder–decoder + global airlight vector; night = contrast attention) | a 36-candidate prior search bought only 0.04 CKA | intervention still flat |
| `rich_proj` fed **into** each expert | expert output was 10–21% of the always-on path | still flat |
| `hard_mask` straight-through | magnitude was tied to gate confidence | still flat |
| `init_gain 0.1` instead of zero-init | at zero the gate gets **no** detection gradient (measured 0.0) | still flat |
| paired restoration loss vs the clear twin, applied to the block SUM | an objective the bypass cannot absorb — except it could | **gain is negative**; fixed in `cond3i` by applying it per expert |

**The restoration loss did not do its job.** Measured on the corrected target
(the full block output on the clear twin, matching what training optimised):

| data | clear branch | fog branch | night branch | winner |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| fog2 | **+0.0172** | −0.0368 | −0.0247 | **clear** |
| night | **+0.0226** | −0.0588 | **−0.0095** | **clear** |

The specialisation table is *inverted*: the plain `clear` branch is the best
restorer on both degraded conditions, and the fog branch is the worst. The
training loss fell (0.576 → 0.245) while the thing it was supposed to cause did
not happen.

## The diagnosis, and it is now specific

The block is a **residual**, not a **mixture**:

```
out = proj(x) + shared(x) + Σ_i  p_i · expert_i(x)
      └──────── always competent ────────┘   └── optional addition ──┘
```

The always-on path alone minimises the detection loss. Everything added to it is
optional by construction, and optimisation leaves optional things small. No
amount of architecture, initialisation, or auxiliary loss changes that, which is
what four designs have now demonstrated — including one (`cond3i`) whose
auxiliary losses provably did what they were designed to do.

**AW-MoE** (Lin et al., in the papers folder) does not have this problem, because
each expert owns a **head** and pays its **own** detection loss:

```
L_CW = Σ_{w ∈ S}  P_w · L_w(WSE_w)
```

An expert cannot be ignored when nothing else computes its output. They also
freeze the shared trunk and initialise every expert by copying a *pretrained*
branch, rather than from zero.

## What I would do next, in order

0. **`cond3j`** (no shared branch) is running — the last cheap test of the
   residual diagnosis.
1. **Measure the seed-noise floor.** 3 seeds of one config. We quote differences
   of 0.004–0.010 mAP and have never measured what a seed alone does. Without it
   nothing in the benchmark table above is interpretable. ~1.5 h.
2. **Per-expert heads + confidence-weighted loss** (AW-MoE Eq. 8). The only
   remaining change that makes an expert non-optional *by construction*.
3. **If that also fails, report the negative result.** It is a clean, well-
   evidenced one: *condition-gated residual MoE at the neck does not help a
   small remote-sensing detector, and here is the measurement chain that shows
   why.* That is a publishable thesis finding, and it is honest.

## What I would stop doing

Tuning the router. It has been solved since the mosaic fix, and four
interventions have improved it without moving accuracy once. Also: judging
expert diversity by CKA — it disagrees with cosine on the same tensors
(0.82–0.98 vs +0.02–0.06) and neither predicts the intervention.

## The small-object ceiling, still unaddressed

Independent of the MoE, and larger than anything it could fix:

| < 8 px | 8–16 px | 16–32 px | 32–64 px | ≥ 64 px |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **100% missed** | **63.7%** | 16.8% | 12.9% | 9.6% |

98% of misses produce no box at any confidence. Only 1.9% are class confusion.
The levers are a P2/stride-4 head and training resolution — not routing, and not
NWD, which was tried twice and traded small-object recall for aggregate mAP both
times.
